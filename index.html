The best of Arch, already set up.

Arch Linux is famous for two things: it's fast and always current, and it takes a whole weekend to configure. Omarchy keeps the first part and does the second for you. Hyprland for windows, Quickshell for the desktop itself, the best apps in every lane — hundreds of small decisions, made once so you don't have to.

"Opinionated" just means we picked. Every setting is still a plain file you can open and change, so start with ours and make it yours whenever you feel like it.

[ 01 ]

Windows that organize themselves

New windows tile neatly instead of piling up. No dragging, no resizing, no clutter. It sounds small. You'll miss it on every other computer.

[ 02 ]

Fast, even on old hardware

Linux runs light and Omarchy keeps it that way. Laptops you'd retired suddenly feel useful again.

22 themes, one keystroke

Switch the whole look — wallpaper, colors, even the browser — with a single shortcut. Twenty-two come built in, and the community has hundreds more.

Everything one keypress away

Hit the Super key and start typing. Apps, settings, files — the Omarchy menu finds them, so you never have to remember where anything lives.

Apps, ready on day one

One command updates everything, with plain-language prompts along the way. Anything break? Roll back on the next reboot and carry on.

Is it for you?

Made for you if…

  • You want a computer that's already set up, but you like the idea of Linux
  • You'd rather have great defaults than a hundred settings screens
  • You live in a browser, a terminal, or a text editor
  • You want your desktop to look as good as it runs

Probably skip it if…

  • One Windows app runs your work life — keep a foot in both worlds with dual boot
  • Your must-play games need anti-cheat that doesn't support Linux
  • You'd rather configure every detail yourself — other distros love that too
  • You want a desktop with no personality. This one has opinions.

Questions people actually ask.

Will it erase my disk?

Only if you tell it to. The installer can take the whole drive or share it with what's already there, and dual booting is covered step by step in the manual.

What kind of computer do I need?

Any x86_64 machine from the last decade — an old ThinkPad is the classic choice. 8 GB of memory and 20 GB of disk are comfortable. Intel and AMD graphics work out of the box; NVIDIA works too, with a driver install.

I've never used Linux. Really, is this for me?

Yes — it's built for exactly you. Nothing needs configuring on day one, the manual explains the keyboard shortcuts as you go, and the Discord answers beginner questions without condescension.

Can I keep Windows around?

You can dual boot, and the manual walks through it. That said, most people find they stop booting into the other one.

What about my Windows apps?

Some run fine through compatibility tools, some don't. If one app is mission-critical, check how it fares before you switch — or keep it on a second machine.

Can I game on it?

A lot of games run great, especially through Steam. The holdouts are titles whose anti-cheat doesn't support Linux — check your favorites first.

What does "opinionated" actually mean?

It means the defaults are chosen on purpose: one window manager, one set of apps, one way of doing things. You can change anything, but you never have to.

Is it really free?

Free as in costs nothing and is locked by nothing — MIT licensed. Development is funded by patrons and the Omacom Foundation.
